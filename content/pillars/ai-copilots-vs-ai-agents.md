## Expanded FAQ

### Is a copilot an agent?
Not fully; copilots assist rather than autonomously execute multi-step goals.

### Which should I adopt first?
Copilots. They are lower risk and deliver quick productivity wins.

### Can agents work without humans?
For bounded tasks yes, but keep oversight and rollback.

### What about compliance?
Agents need stronger logging and DPDP controls than copilots.

### Do they cost the same?
Agents often cost more due to tool usage and orchestration.

## Extended Analysis: Adoption Path for Indian Teams

### Productivity First
Copilots deliver fast, visible productivity gains inside the tools your team already uses. They are the safer first step and build trust in AI-assisted work.

### Automation Second
Agents deliver step-change automation but need more setup, stronger guardrails, and clearer success metrics. Treat agents as the second phase after copilots have proven the workflow is worth automating.

### Cost Trajectory
Copilots are usually per-seat SaaS. Agents add usage and integration cost. Plan the budget accordingly and measure the incremental value of each layer.

### Compliance Hierarchy
Copilots suggest; agents act. That means agents need tighter access controls, logging, and human-override paths. Under DPDP, both need consent, but agents need it at every tool action.

### People and Change Management
Copilots reduce training friction because they assist rather than replace. Agents require process redesign. Indian teams should expect more change management with agents.
