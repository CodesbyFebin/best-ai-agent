# Comparison Methodology

Comparison pages are the most-trusted and the most-misused artefacts on a research site. This page documents how we build comparison pages, what evidence windows we require, and how we handle the reverse-direction ("X vs. Y") slug problem.

## When we will publish a comparison page

We publish a comparison page only when both products under review have:

- A primary source we can cite for at least the head-to-head criteria.
- Equivalent evidence windows (we do not compare today's evidence for product A against last year's evidence for product B).
- Comparable surface area in the comparison (we do not compare an enterprise plan against a hobby plan without saying so explicitly).

When one side has thin evidence we either refuse to publish the page, narrow the comparison scope, or move the page to `needs_more_research`.

## Evidence window parity

Every comparison page declares an evidence window (the date range of the receipts we relied on). If the windows are not equivalent — for example because product A was last reviewed on 2026-07-12 and product B was last reviewed on 2025-11-04 — we either re-verify the older side or note the asymmetry in the methodology section.

We do not silently inherit older evidence to make a comparison look fresher than it is.

## Declared weights and criteria

Every comparison page publishes:

- The criteria being compared (e.g. reasoning, tool use, latency, India fit, enterprise scalability).
- The weight assigned to each criterion.
- The source for each numeric value.
- The freshness of each numeric value.

The criteria and weights are declared in the page's methodology section. We do not change weights to force a desired verdict.

## Scenario recommendations

Comparison pages conclude with scenario recommendations ("choose A when X, choose B when Y"). The scenarios are evidence-backed; they are not sales pitches for either vendor.

## Reverse-route canonicalization

For every comparison we choose one canonical direction ("A vs. B" wins, "B vs. A" 301s to "A vs. B"). The reverse-direction routes are listed in `src/content/registry/content-registry.ts` under `CANONICAL_REDIRECTS` and produce 301 status codes at the routing layer.

The canonical direction rule applies regardless of search volume. We do not swap canonical direction to chase a keyword.

## What comparison pages do not do

- We do not declare a winner without a methodology. "X is the best" is rejected unless the methodology is published on the same page.
- We do not invent `AggregateRating`, `Review`, or numeric review-count schema. We never mark up a comparison page with a score that is not visible on the page.
- We do not include affiliate links inside the verdict. Affiliate disclosure lives on [](/trust/affiliate-disclosure) and is reproduced on every page that links to a commercial partner.
