# Rating Methodology

Numeric scores appear on entity profiles and comparison pages. This page documents how those scores are computed, what they do and do not mean, and what we refuse to score.

## Where scores come from

Scores on this site are derived by the `calculateQualityScore` function in `src/data/evidenceSchema.ts`. The function aggregates six signals into a 0-100 score:

| Component | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|-------:|------------------|
| `evidence` | 25% | Share of material claims with at least one receipt |
| `authority` | 20% | Average authority of cited sources (primary = 100, secondary = 75, community = 50) |
| `freshness` | 15% | Share of receipts whose freshness date is within 30 days |
| `contradictionRisk` | 15% | Inverse of contradictions in the claim ledger |
| `intentSatisfaction` | 10% | Match between the page body and the page's declared search intent |
| `entityCoverage` | 15% | Coverage of entities referenced by the page |

The function returns an `overall` score that is rounded to the nearest integer and stored on the entity profile. The function returns `false` for `passesQualityGate` until evidence coverage, source authority, and overall score all clear their declared thresholds.

## What scores never mean

- A score is **not** a verdict. A high score means the entity profile has strong evidence coverage; it does not mean the entity is "good" for your use case. The verdict belongs to the comparison and review pages, where evidence windows and review methodology are declared.
- A score is **not** a ranking. We do not publish score-based leaderboards except where the leaderboard is a declared methodology with declared weights.
- A score is **not** durable. Scores decay as evidence ages. A page in `refresh_required` shows the score with a freshness warning, not as if it were current.

## What we refuse to score

We refuse to publish numeric scores for:

- Adoption or popularity (we have no audited measurement).
- "Best in class" verdicts without a declared methodology and weight set.
- Vendor trust or security certification without access to the certification document.
- User satisfaction or review scores without a verified sample and methodology.
- Latency, uptime, or cost without our own measurement or a cited primary source.

When a metric is unavailable we say so plainly. We do not synthesize a number to fill a table cell.

## Declaration on every comparison page

Comparison pages declare:

- The scoring dimensions and their weights.
- The evidence window for each product.
- Any vendor relationship that could influence the score (with reference to [](/trust/affiliate-disclosure)).
- The freshness of the underlying data.

The declaration lives in the methodology section of every comparison page and is reproduced from [](/trust/comparison-methodology).
