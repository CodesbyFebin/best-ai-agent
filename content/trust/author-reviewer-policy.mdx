# Author and Reviewer Identification Policy

Every published page on BestAIAgent.in identifies an author (where named) and a reviewer (always named). This page documents the rules we apply.

## What we publish on every page

Every published page includes:

- **Author** — the writer's name, or "BestAIAgent.in editorial team" for unattributed pieces.
- **Reviewer** — the named reviewer who checked the page against [](/trust/editorial-methodology).
- **Last reviewed** — the ISO 8601 date the page was last reviewed for accuracy.
- **Next scheduled review** — the ISO 8601 date the next review is due.

The author and reviewer are recorded in the page frontmatter under `author`, `reviewer`, `lastReviewed`, and `nextReviewDue`.

## Author identification rules

- Authors who write under their real name must have a public bio on this site linking to their other work. Pseudonymous authors are permitted for personal-safety reasons but must be approved by the editorial board.
- Authors are responsible for the prose, the structure, and the accuracy of the claims they attribute to other people.
- Authors may not review their own pages. The reviewer is always a different person.

## Reviewer requirements

The reviewer:

- Checks the page against [](/trust/editorial-methodology).
- Verifies that every material claim has a receipt in `src/content/registry/evidence-ledger.ts`.
- Verifies that no claim contradicts an existing receipt.
- Confirms that the methodology declared on the page matches the rules in [](/trust/comparison-methodology) or [](/trust/rating-methodology) where applicable.
- Adds their name to the page frontmatter before publication.

The reviewer is the editorial board for methodology pages and a domain specialist (or the editorial board if no specialist is available) for product pages.

## When the reviewer is the editorial board

For methodology pages and pages with no product claim, the reviewer is the BestAIAgent.in editorial board. The board is identified on [](/about) and its decisions are public.

## Why authorship and review are non-negotiable

Editorial transparency is not a stylistic preference; it is the foundation of trust on a research site. Three reasons we treat author and reviewer identification as mandatory:

1. **Accountability.** A named reviewer can be asked to defend a decision. An anonymous page cannot be held to account.
2. **Calibration.** Future reviewers can compare their verdict against prior reviewer decisions and identify drift.
3. **Audit.** When a correction is filed in [](/trust/corrections), the author and reviewer at the time of publication are recorded, so the chain of responsibility is recoverable.
