# Source Classification

Every evidence receipt carries an `authority` field: `primary`, `secondary`, or `community`. The classification determines which evidence gate the receipt satisfies and how the receipt is weighted by [](/trust/rating-methodology). This page documents how we classify each tier.

## Primary

A source is `primary` when it originates the claim or certifies the underlying fact. Examples on this site:

- Vendor pricing pages (e.g. `cursor.com/pricing`, `anthropic.com/pricing`).
- Vendor product / capability documentation.
- Vendor security and compliance pages.
- Official protocol specifications (e.g. `modelcontextprotocol.io/specification`).
- Statutes, regulations, and official government publications (e.g. the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023).
- Official repositories and the files they host.

A primary source may still be wrong. We do not treat `primary` as proof; we treat it as the strongest class of source we can cite.

## Secondary

A source is `secondary` when it reports on, analyses, or independently tests a primary claim. Examples on this site:

- Reputable technology publications with editorial standards.
- Independent benchmark or testing organisations.
- Conference papers and peer-reviewed research.
- Standards bodies and their published standards.

Secondary sources are weighted below primary sources in the rating methodology and cannot alone satisfy a `STANDARD` gate for material claims.

## Community

A source is `community` when it is anecdotal or crowd-supplied. Examples on this site:

- Forum threads and discussion boards.
- Personal blog posts without editorial standards.
- Social media posts.
- Conference talks without published proceedings.

Community sources may be cited to illustrate a use case or a reported experience but cannot satisfy any evidence gate on their own. When we cite a community source we mark it as anecdotal.

## Interactions with evidence gates

The `STANDARD`, `COMPARISON`, and `CRITICAL` gates have minimum authority requirements:

- `STANDARD` requires 1+ primary source.
- `COMPARISON` requires 2+ primary sources (one per product side).
- `CRITICAL` requires 2+ primary OR 1 primary + 2 secondary.

A claim supported only by secondary or community sources is reclassified as `unresolved` in the claim ledger, regardless of how confident the model appears to be.

## Source classification register

The canonical source classification register lives in `src/content/registry/evidence-ledger.ts`. Each entry carries:

- `url` (canonical URL of the source)
- `publisher` (entity that controls the source)
- `authority` (`primary`, `secondary`, `community`)
- `retrievedAt` (ISO 8601 date)

When we change a source's classification we log the change in [](/trust/corrections).
