# Editorial Methodology

BestAIAgent.in publishes entity and topic authority content about AI agents for Indian developers, startups, enterprises, and regulated organizations. This page documents how we decide what to publish, what we refuse to publish, who is responsible for each page, and how we correct mistakes.

## What this page covers

- Editorial independence and affiliate disclosure
- Source hierarchy and authority classification
- Page classes and their review requirements
- Author and reviewer identification policy
- Corrections and version history
- When we will not publish a page

## Editorial independence

BestAIAgent.in is editorially independent. Vendor relationships, affiliate programs, and commercial partnerships do not determine which products we cover, the order in which we cover them, the verdicts we reach, or the framing of any review. Affiliate disclosure is published at [](/trust/affiliate-disclosure) and on every page that links to a commercial partner.

We pay for our own product evaluations whenever feasible. Where a vendor provides a temporary licence for testing, we disclose that fact on the affected review page in the "How we tested" section.

## Source hierarchy

We classify sources by authority before assigning evidence class:

| Class | Definition | Examples |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Primary** | Vendor / official / statutory source that originates the claim | Vendor pricing pages, official protocol specifications, statutes, official repositories |
| **Secondary** | Independent reporting, peer-reviewed research, official testing bodies | Reputable technology publications, standards bodies, conference papers |
| **Community** | Anecdotal or community-supplied | Forum threads, blog posts, conference talks without published proceedings |

For any material claim about price, capability, compliance, or compatibility we require at least one primary source. Comparison pages require equivalent primary sources for both products under review. Legal, medical, financial, and security claims require multiple authoritative sources plus human review — see [](/trust/comparison-methodology) and the relevant methodology pages.

## Page classes and review requirements

We use the following page classes. The review depth depends on the risk of the claim being wrong.

- **Pillar** — synthesizes a domain. Requires primary-source evidence for every material claim and editorial review before publication.
- **Cluster** — addresses a specific question inside a pillar. Requires primary-source evidence and editorial review.
- **Comparison** — head-to-head product review. Requires equivalent evidence windows for both products. See [](/trust/comparison-methodology).
- **Review** — single-product review. Requires a tested or desk-researched evaluation method declared up front.
- **Pricing** — current vendor pricing. Requires primary-source evidence and a freshness window. See [](/trust/freshness-policy).
- **Methodology** — this page and its siblings. Reviewed before publication and re-reviewed when underlying policy changes.

## Author and reviewer identification

Every published page identifies the author (where named) and the reviewer (always named). We publish corrections when material errors are identified, even if the page has aged off the homepage. The corrections log lives at [](/trust/corrections).

## When we will not publish a page

We refuse to publish pages when:

- The page exists primarily to rank for a keyword rather than answer a real user question.
- The required evidence is not available from a primary source.
- The vendor refuses to confirm or deny a claim that materially affects the user's decision.
- The page duplicates an existing page with only reordered adjectives.
- The page would require us to certify a vendor's compliance or security without access to verifiable evidence.

We quarantine such pages as `quarantined_template` rather than delete them, so the candidate inventory is auditable.
